# Sex and autism share cell type specific effects on cortical gene expression


Authors: Gabrielle Dugan 1,2, Alex R. DeCasien 2, Ramin Ali Marandi Ghoddousi 3, Rachel Smith 4, Siyuan Liu 1, Pavan Auluck 5, Stefano Marenco 5, Daniel Geschwind 3, Jason P. Lerch 6,7,8, Armin Raznahan 1

## Abstract: 

Potent sex effects on the prevalence and presentation of autism could theoretically reflect overlapping neurobiology, but tests for this at the level of brain gene expression have so far been limited to bulk tissue – overlooking recently described cell type heterogeneity. Here, we use recently available single-nucleus RNA-sequencing data, to test if regional, cell type-specific effects of sex on gene expression overlap with those of autism (using schizophrenia as a comparison condition).  Cellular gene expression effects were estimated for sex across six cortical regions (169 samples, 30 individuals), and for autism (63 individuals, 33 cases), and schizophrenia (140 individuals, 65 cases) in frontal cortex. Genes with statistically significant sex effects on gene expression showed a stronger significant overlap with those differentially expressed in autism than schizophrenia. Multivariate analyses revealed a significant shared axis of cell type transcriptomic effects between sex and autism (but not schizophrenia). This axis reveals a protein-protein interaction network that is: enriched for autism risk genes and roles in synaptic biology; downregulated in autism across cell types; and female-biased in fusiform neurons, but male-biased in angular gyrus glial cells. Thus, the transcriptomic signature of autism as measured in frontal cortex cell types shows cell type and brain region specific alignments with sex effects on gene expression – pointing to a complexity in transcriptomic overlaps that informs theoretical models for sex modulation of autism liability.
